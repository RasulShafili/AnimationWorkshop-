## Fact notes (all real; the story around them is fiction)
- Speed of sound in seawater ≈ 1,480–1,530 m/s (≈4.3× air).
- SOFAR (deep sound) channel: sound can travel thousands of km.
- The Bloop (1997) was later attributed to icequakes (NOAA).
- Snapping shrimp: cavitation bubble collapse, very loud (shrimp "sonoluminescence"/flash is documented).
- Blue whale heart rate: 4–8 bpm on dives, as low as 2 bpm; 25–37 bpm after surfacing (Goldbogen et al., PNAS 2019).
- Ghost nets: lost/abandoned fishing gear keeps entangling animals for years.
- Large-whale disentanglement teams cut lines from small boats using knives on long poles.
- Upsweep: recorded since 1991 on NOAA hydrophones near ~54°S 140°W; thought volcanic; not confirmed.
  ("It's moving" is the fictional Part-3 hook.)
