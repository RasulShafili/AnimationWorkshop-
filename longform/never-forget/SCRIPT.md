# What If You Could Never Forget Anything?
Long-form YouTube 16:9, target ~8:30 (over 8:00 → mid-roll ads). New project, NOT a sequel: new hero, new world,
new look. Second person ("you"). Part 1 of a possible series ("The Missing Day" = Part 2 hook).

Why this topic (research 2026-11-14, see NOTES.md): long-form outliers in the story-"what if" niche are mind/perception
powers with personal stakes (IFNation "Hear Your Thoughts" 19.7× its channel median, Ifverse "500 IQ" 17.6×,
"Woke Up in Kindergarten With All Your Memories" 5.3×). "Remember everything" has demand (Infographics 479k,
OTHERWISE "Reborn With All Your Memories" 834k) but no strong animated *story* version, and it is pure science under
the hood (HSAM, Ebbinghaus, Loftus, Shereshevsky, music memory in Alzheimer's) → our brand: story + real facts.

## Fact notes (all real; the story is fiction)
- Loftus & Palmer (1974): verb wording ("smashed"/"hit") changed speed estimates; memory is reconstructive.
- Ebbinghaus (1885): forgetting curve, steep loss within hours/days without review.
- HSAM: Parker, Cahill & McGaugh (2006) first case ("AJ", Jill Price); UC Irvine has confirmed only a few dozen
  (~60) people.
- Infantile amnesia: earliest memories typically from ~age 2.5–3.5.
- Method of loci: ancient Greek/Roman technique (Simonides legend ~500 BC); used by memory champions; speed-cards
  records are under 15 s (we say "under twenty seconds" to stay safely true).
- Card counting: legal in the US; casinos may refuse service/ban counters.
- Shereshevsky (A. R. Luria, "The Mind of a Mnemonist", 1968): tried writing things down and burning the paper to
  forget; it did not work.
- Forgetting as adaptive: Richards & Frankland, Neuron 2017.
- Music memory relatively spared in Alzheimer's: Jacobsen et al., Brain 2015.
- The pill that makes you forget, the missing day: fiction (clearly part of the story).
