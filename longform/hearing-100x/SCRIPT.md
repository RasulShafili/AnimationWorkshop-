# What If Your Hearing Was 100x Better?
Long-form YouTube, 16:9 1920×1080, ~7:30. Cinematic 2D (silhouette characters with expressive eyes, moody dusk
lighting, rim light, layered detailed backgrounds, glow, depth blur, constantly moving camera). OUR OWN hero design:
a teenager with big over-ear headphones around the neck (the story's key prop), mustard raincoat — not a copy of any
existing channel's character.
Voices: narrator = Kokoro am_fenrir (second person, "you"). Side characters: Mia (best friend, af_heart),
Dad (bm_george), Rescue chief (am_michael), Stranger on the phone (bf_emma).
Music: original ambient score (piano + strings pads, our own), quiet under the voice; SFX are the star of this
video — every sound the hero hears is heard by the viewer.
Retention rules used (from the Ifverse reference analysis): action in second 1, the title question at ~0:30,
a new "wow" every 60–90 s, real facts as twists, a cost, an emotional choice at the climax, a cliffhanger into Part 2,
comment question at the end. Chapters in the description.

## 0:00 — The heartbeat through the wall  (cold open)
VISUAL: 3 a.m., dark bedroom, rain on the window. Hero's eyes snap open. Camera pushes through the wall: an
x-ray-style "sound view" shows the old neighbour in the next flat, his heartbeat as a glowing pulse.
- NARRATOR: It's 3 a.m. The whole building is asleep. But you're wide awake… because you can hear your neighbour's heart.
- SFX: thump… thump… thump-thump… thump. Then a long flat silence.
- NARRATOR: And it just skipped. Twice.
VISUAL: Hero grabs the phone, runs down the stairwell in pyjamas, pounds on the door. Ambulance lights paint the street.
- NARRATOR: You call an ambulance before he even knows something's wrong.
- PARAMEDIC: Another ten minutes and we'd have lost him. Who called us?
- NARRATOR: A teenager… two walls away.
TITLE CARD (0:30): WHAT IF YOUR HEARING WAS 100× BETTER?

## 0:40 — How it works
VISUAL: Infographic over the hero's head: sound waves → ear → brain; a dial from WHISPER to JET ENGINE.
- NARRATOR: Here's the deal. Your ears pick up sounds a hundred times quieter than anyone else's.
- NARRATOR: A whisper across a football field? Clear as a phone call. A mosquito three rooms away? It sounds like a drone.
- NARRATOR: And here's the scary part. Your ears were already close to the limit of physics.
- VISUAL: zoom into the air: tiny molecules bouncing off an eardrum like hail.
- NARRATOR: Air is made of molecules, and they never stop bumping into your eardrum. Normal ears barely miss that noise. Yours don't.
- SFX: a soft, endless hiss fades in… and stays (very quiet bed for the rest of the video).
- NARRATOR: So even in total silence… there's a hiss. Forever.

## 2:30 — The lie detector
VISUAL: Night, Dad's poker game in a smoky kitchen. Five players, cards, chips. Hero watches from the doorway.
- NARRATOR: Then you notice something nobody else can hear. When people lie… their hearts speed up.
- VISUAL: each player gets a glowing heartbeat icon; one beats faster when he says "I've got nothing."
- NARRATOR: He says he's got nothing. His heart says he's got a full house.
- NARRATOR: You win eleven games in a row.

## 3:20 — The human sonar
VISUAL: News montage → an earthquake hits a nearby town: collapsed buildings in dust, golden late light, rescuers.
- NARRATOR: Three months later, an earthquake hits the next town.
- NARRATOR: Rescue teams have a rule: every hour, everyone goes silent… and they listen for tapping.
- VISUAL: rescue chief raising a fist, all machines stop, dust drifting in the silence.
- RESCUE CHIEF: Silence on the site! Everybody listen!
- NARRATOR: That's real. But they can only hear tapping. You can hear heartbeats.
- VISUAL: "sound view" through the rubble: three faint glowing pulses under concrete.
- NARRATOR: Under three metres of concrete… three people. Still alive.
- VISUAL: rescuers dig where the hero points; a little girl's hand reaches up into the light.
- RESCUE CHIEF: How did you know they were there?
- NARRATOR: Within a week, the whole country calls you "the Human Sonar."
- FACT CARD: "Real rescue teams use 'silent periods' and acoustic listening devices to find survivors."

## 4:30 — The price of silence
VISUAL: Hero's room at night, headphones on, eyes open, ceiling. Clock 4:12 a.m.
- NARRATOR: But there's a price.
- NARRATOR: Remember the hiss? It's still there. And now you can also hear your own body.
- SFX: own heartbeat loud, blood rushing, the creak of joints, a stomach gurgle.
- NARRATOR: Your blood rushing. Your joints creaking. Your own heart, all night, like a drum next to your pillow.
- FACT CARD: "In anechoic chambers — the quietest rooms on Earth — people start hearing their own heartbeat and blood."
- VISUAL: a city street: a motorbike passes and the hero doubles over, hands on the headphones.
- NARRATOR: A motorbike feels like a jet engine. A concert would actually hurt you.
- MIA: You okay?
- HERO: Everything is just… so loud.
- NARRATOR: So you move with your dad to a quiet cabin in the mountains. Snow. Pine trees. Finally, almost silence.

## 5:30 — The avalanche  (climax)
VISUAL: Blue-white mountain dawn. A school ski trip on the slope above; then a crack, a white wall racing down.
- NARRATOR: Until one morning… you hear the mountain crack.
- SFX: deep crack, rumble growing to a roar, then silence and falling powder.
- NARRATOR: An avalanche buries a ski trail. One kid is missing. Mia's little brother, Leo.
- VISUAL: rescuers with probes, a helicopter hovering, its rotor wash blasting snow.
- NARRATOR: The rescue helicopter is right above the field. With that noise, you can't hear anything.
- NARRATOR: Under snow, you have about fifteen minutes.
- FACT CARD: "Avalanche survival drops fast after the first 15 minutes."
- VISUAL: the hero looks at the helicopter, then at his headphones. Close-up of his hands.
- NARRATOR: There's one way. Tell the helicopter to land… and take your headphones off. In the open, at full volume.
- NARRATOR: It's going to hurt. Maybe forever.
- HERO (shouting to the chief): Land it! Everyone — silence!
- VISUAL: the helicopter lands; the whole field freezes. Hero takes the headphones off. The screen sound "opens up":
  wind, the hiss, hundreds of heartbeats of rescuers… and under them, one faint, slow pulse.
- NARRATOR: Hundreds of heartbeats around you. And somewhere under the snow… one very slow one.
- VISUAL: the hero runs, falls to his knees, digs with his hands; rescuers join; a mitten, then Leo's face, gasping.
- MIA (crying): He's breathing! He's breathing!
- NARRATOR: Leo is fine.
- VISUAL: later, in the hospital, the hero sits with bandaged ears. Muffled sound.
- NARRATOR: Your ears aren't. The doctors say your hearing will heal… but only to ten times better. Not a hundred.
- NARRATOR: You'd trade it again. Every time.

## Fact notes
- Hearing threshold is near the thermal (Brownian) noise of air; the most sensitive listeners approach it
  (Harrison, arXiv:0910.3170; JASA 2024 comment).
- Anechoic chambers: people report hearing their heartbeat/blood flow.
- Earthquake rescue: "silent periods" + acoustic/seismic listening devices.
- Avalanche burial: survival falls sharply after ~15 min.
- The Bloop: NOAA hydrophones 1997, heard >5,000 km away; attributed to icequakes. ("Came back… heartbeat" is the
  fictional Part-2 hook, clearly fiction.)
