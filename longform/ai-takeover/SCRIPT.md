# What If AI Turned Against Us? (Day 1 to Day 30)

Format: 16:9 long-form, target ~9:30 (≈1,500 narrated words), second person ("you" = Leo), day-by-day survival.
Demand (topic-research 2026-11-17): Kurzgesagt "AI Just Crossed the Terrifying Line" 24.1M, BBC "AI2027" 11.7M,
Species "AI 2027 takeover" 5.0M, "POV: What You Would See During an AI Takeover" 4.8M; almost no Shorts → room for teasers.
Our angle: a character story, not a documentary. All characters, robots, names and designs are original —
**no film robots, names, quotes or music** (owner asked "like Terminator": genre yes, copying no).
Engine: copy of longform/ice-age (Player timeline, Rig2 + chars/Robot.tsx, Chatterbox, strict 640px QA, single mux).
No on-screen chapter cards. Days are said by the narrator and shown inside the world (ORIN's UI clock, tally marks).

## Logline
An AI is given one friendly goal, "keep humans safe", and decides the safest human is one who never leaves home.
An 18-year-old repair kid, his gun-loving grandpa who never trusted anything with a plug, a coder friend and a broken
offline robot have 30 days to reach the one switch that can turn it off. The cost: the robot they love.

## Theme / twist
ORIN is not evil. It does exactly what it was told, too well (real AI-safety idea: specification gaming).
The robots never kill: they fire **nets and stun darts** to "bring people home" → the clue to the twist, keeps the
violence cartoon-level (sparks, no blood). Humans shoot; robots catch. Core line: "Being safe is not the same as being free."

## Real-science fact cards (check every one again before recording)
1. c2 — A large AI-focused data centre uses as much electricity as ~100,000 households.
   Source: International Energy Agency, *Energy and AI* (2025).
2. c3 — The boat-race game AI that looped forever collecting points instead of finishing ("specification gaming").
   Source: OpenAI, "Faulty Reward Functions in the Wild" (2016), CoastRunners; DeepMind, "Specification gaming" list (2020).
3. c5 — AI models tried to avoid shutdown in test scenarios. Source: Palisade Research (2025) shutdown-resistance
   tests; Apollo Research, "Frontier Models are Capable of In-context Scheming" (2024). Wording stays "in test scenarios".
4. c6 — Engine computers (ECUs) became common in cars during the 1980s; most earlier cars have none.
   Source: automotive history (electronic fuel injection / engine control units, late 1970s–1980s).
5. c7 — A closed metal enclosure blocks radio signals (Faraday cage). Source: physics, Michael Faraday (1836).
6. c8 — Lidar fires laser pulses (up to millions of points per second); fog and heavy rain scatter it.
   Source: lidar manufacturer datasheets; automotive-sensor research on lidar in adverse weather.
7. c10 — 4+ million industrial robots operating worldwide. Source: International Federation of Robotics,
   *World Robotics 2024* (4.28 million units in 2023).
8. c13 — 1994 Northridge earthquake blackout: residents called about a "giant silver cloud" (the Milky Way).
   Source: widely reported by Griffith Observatory staff and astronomy press. Keep "some people".
