# What If You Teleported Every Time You Thought of a Place? (draft v1, 2026-11-22)
Style: story-Short look (stick3d characters, rich sets, moving camera, one-word captions), 16:9, ~7.5 min.
Mix of story + explainer: ONE hero ("you") + 3 small roles (Sam = best friend, Mom, Mr. Kent = teacher).
Every 20–30 s a NEW place or danger; every chapter ends on a mini-cliffhanger. Real science in 1–2 lines per
chapter, said inside the story (no fact cards except quick 2D numbers).

## 0. Cold open (0:00–0:25)
N: Your teacher says, "Imagine you're standing on the Moon." And then you are.
N: No air. No sound. Your spit starts to boil on your tongue. You have about fifteen seconds before you pass out.
N: You squeeze your eyes shut and think of the only place you can picture. Your bedroom.
N: Pop. You're on your carpet, gasping, with frost on your eyelashes.
N: So… what if you teleported every time you thought of a place?

## 1. Day one: the rule (0:25–1:15)
N: It takes you one morning to figure it out. You think "fridge." You're at the fridge. You think "bathroom." Bathroom.
N: Not "go there." Just picture it clearly, and you're there. No flash, no sound. Just a little pop in your ears.
N: So you do what anyone would do. Lunch in Tokyo.
N: Pop. It's pitch black. Every shop is closed. Because when it's noon at your house, it's two in the morning in Tokyo.
N: A very confused cat stares at you. You stare back. You go home and eat cereal.
SAM: Wait. You can go ANYWHERE? Take me to Hawaii!
N: You try. Sam doesn't come. Only you, your clothes, and whatever's in your pockets.
N: And that's when you realize the real problem. You can't turn it off.

## 2. Don't think about it (1:15–2:05)
N: Try this. For the next five seconds, do NOT think about a white bear.
N: You just thought about a white bear.
N: In 1987, a psychologist named Daniel Wegner asked people to not think about a white bear for five minutes.
N: They thought about it more than once a minute. And when they were allowed to think about it again, it came back even stronger.
N: Your brain checks if you're still not thinking about the thing… by thinking about the thing.
MR. KENT: Okay class, page forty. The Sahara Desert.
N: Pop. Sand so hot it burns through your socks. A camel looks at you like you owe it money.
N: You think "classroom" so hard you land on your teacher's desk.
MR. KENT: …Detention.

## 3. The list of places you must never picture (2:05–3:35)
N: So you make a list. Places you are never, ever allowed to think about.
N: Number one. The top of Mount Everest. Up there, every breath has about a third of the oxygen you're used to. Pilots call
   it the death zone, because you have a minute or two before your brain starts shutting down.
N: Number two. Point Nemo. The loneliest spot in the ocean. The nearest land is over two thousand kilometres away.
   Some days the closest people to you are the astronauts on the space station, flying four hundred kilometres over your head.
N: Number three. Antarctica. The coldest temperature ever recorded there was minus eighty-nine degrees.
N: Number four. The lion enclosure at the zoo. This one isn't science. It's just lions.
N: The list is the problem. Every time you read it… you picture it.
N: Pop. Snow. Pop. Lions. Pop. Snow again. You're flicking around the planet like a broken TV remote.
N: Your mom finds you in the kitchen, soaking wet, holding a penguin feather.
MOM: I'm not even going to ask.

## 4. The safe thought (3:35–4:25)
N: Wegner found a trick, though. Don't fight the thought. Replace it.
N: People who were told to think about a red car instead of the white bear thought about the bear way less.
N: So you pick your red car. Your bedroom. Every crack in the ceiling. The blue lamp. The sock under the bed.
N: You practise it a hundred times a day. Sam yells random places at you.
SAM: Volcano! Shark tank! Your crush's house!
N: Pop… pop… and pop. Bedroom. Bedroom. Bedroom. By Friday, your brain jumps home before it even finishes the scary thought.
N: You did it. You're safe. Until the sun goes down.

## 5. Night (4:25–5:35)
N: Because there's one time you can't control your thoughts at all. When you're asleep.
N: You dream four to six times every night. And every dream has a place in it.
N: You wake up on a beach in your pyjamas. On top of a bus. In a supermarket freezer, next to the frozen peas.
N: So you decide to stop sleeping.
N: Bad idea. After about eighteen hours awake, your brain works like it's had a couple of drinks. After a full day, like
   someone who's legally drunk. Your thoughts get slow and slippery. Exactly what you can't afford.
N: On night three, you fall asleep in the bath, and wake up in the middle of a football stadium, during the game.
N: Sixty thousand people. One kid in a towel.
N: So you make a deal with your brain. You can dream… but every dream starts and ends in your bedroom.

## 6. The documentary (5:35–6:35)
N: Two weeks later, you're at Sam's house. Sam turns on the TV.
N: A documentary about the deepest place on Earth. The Mariana Trench.
N: Down there, the water presses on you with about eight tons on every square inch. Nothing like you survives a second.
N: You see the black water on the screen. Your brain starts to build the picture.
SAM: Hey… are you okay? You're kind of… flickering.
N: Your ears pop. Your skin goes cold. For one tiny moment, the room fills with dark blue.
N: And then — bedroom. Your brain jumped home before the thought was even finished.
N: You lie on your floor, staring at the crack in the ceiling, laughing like an idiot. The training worked.

## 7. What it's really for (6:35–7:30)
N: After that, you stop being scared of it. You start being careful with it.
N: You can only go where you can picture. So you start learning places. Real ones.
N: The hospital across town, every hallway. The fire station. Your grandma's kitchen, three hundred kilometres away.
N: When grandma falls and can't reach the phone, you're the first one there. Faster than any ambulance.
N: It turns out the power was never about going anywhere. It was about knowing exactly where you are… and where you want to be.
N: Except the Moon. You're never thinking about the Moon again.
MR. KENT: Okay class, today we're learning about… the solar system.
N: Oh no.
