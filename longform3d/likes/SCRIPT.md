# What If You Could See How Much Everyone Likes You?

Format: 16:9 long-form, 3D toon (lib3d + sets3d + thoughts engine), target 8:30+ (≈1,350 spoken words), second person
("you" = Ben), Day 1 → Day 30. Simple Ifology-style animation; science lines cut to 2D motion graphics (memory

## Logline
A 13-year-old wakes up seeing a glowing number above every head: how much that person likes HIM. His crush: 3%.
The school's scariest kid: 91%. His best friend: 62% and falling. For thirty days he learns what moves the numbers —
and on the last day he finally sees the one number he never thought to check: his own.

## Theme
People like you more than you think (the liking gap), the way to be liked is to show up and be kind (mere exposure,
reciprocity) — and the number that matters most is how much you like yourself.

- Colours: 0–30 red, 31–69 yellow, 70–100 green. A change gets a little "tick" sound + sparkle.

## Real facts used (description + 2D graphics)
- Liking gap — Boothby, Cooney, Sandstrom & Clark, Psychological Science (2018), Cornell/Yale: after conversations
  people consistently underestimated how much their partner liked them.
- Mere exposure effect — Zajonc (1968); classroom study Moreland & Beach (1992): a student who attended more lectures
  (never interacting) was rated more familiar, attractive and likable.
- Reciprocity of liking — we tend to like people who we think like us (e.g., Montoya & Insko meta-analysis, 2008).
